ETG Platform

Field-service operations behind one decision gate.

A field-service platform for an Australian electrical, security and telecom group: admin, client, supplier and technician portals over one API, built to stop about $5k a week lost to unbilled supplier costs and to billing scheduled rather than actual hours.

01 · The brief

Stop margin leakage before work ships.

The group runs two businesses that share customers at the same sites. Two costs drove the build: jobs billed from the calendar instead of clocked time, and supplier invoices that never got matched, an estimated $5k a week of leakage. The platform answers with enforcement: a job cannot reach "ready to invoice" until labour is reconciled to clocked time and supplier costs are matched to bank transactions.

  • Five portals: operations back office, technician, client, subcontractor, and tokenised public links for quotes, invoices and feedback
  • Quote to cash: work requests, quotes, projects, field jobs, service tickets, receivables and retentions
  • Procurement and cost: purchase orders, supplier invoices and credit notes, stock and the bank feed
  • Supplier bills: emailed bills are read by supplier-specific PDF readers, or prefilled by the Claude API, and a person approves each one
  • Payments: supplier bills paid through Airwallex transfers, with a second approver above $1,000
  • Workforce: calendar, availability, timesheets, compliance, award rules and field checklists
  • Governance: approvals, alerts, enforcement events, SLA targets and the audit log

Key decisions

  1. 01One pure function, evaluateDecision(), gates every dispatch, closeout and invoice transition and returns structured failure codes; every block and manager override is logged with who and why
  2. 02Layers enforced by lint: the frontend never imports Prisma and routers reach the database only through repositories, so a violation fails CI
  3. 03Two businesses that share customers modelled in the schema from the start, with ownership routing on service tickets
  4. 04Money held as BigInt integer cents on an ex-GST basis, so no financial path uses floating point
03 · My part

What I worked on.

  • Designed the monorepo and its data model: a Next.js frontend, a Fastify and tRPC API and a BullMQ worker over one PostgreSQL schema for dispatch, timesheets, assets, quoting and billing.
  • Enforcement-first decision engine: one pure, unit-tested gate checks every dispatch, closeout and invoice step and logs each block and manager override; a job cannot be invoiced until its supplier costs match the bank feed.
  • Shared inbox and AI bill reading over Gmail: every email links to its job, quote or invoice, and supplier bills are parsed by per-supplier PDF readers or prefilled through the Claude API for a person to approve.
  • Integration layer: typed adapters for more than ten external systems, including trade supplier portals with no public API; supplier bills are paid through Airwallex transfers with a second approver on larger payments.
  • Delivery: GitHub Actions deploys to Railway and Vercel pinned to one commit, production smoke tests and an hourly health check, backed by 52,000+ automated tests run in CI.
  • Five role-scoped portals over one API: internal operations, technician mobile, client and subcontractor self-service, and tokenised public links for quotes, invoices and feedback.
  • Credential security: Google Cloud KMS envelope encryption, Argon2 hashing, mandatory two-factor sign-in and procedure-level permissions.
  • Shipped through the team's multi-agent Claude Code workflow: layer-author and read-only auditor subagents in parallel git worktrees, with lint rules, permission rules and hooks that block rule violations, production database writes and unverified work.
  • AI guardrails as code: a canonical rules file with path-scoped rules, auditors on the plan for any money, permission or schema change, and a mistakes log that feeds new rules and gates.
